FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What is LPG?

LPG (liquefied petroleum gas) is a mixture of propane and butane, liquefied under moderate pressure. It is used for cooking, heating, industry, autogas and as a petrochemical feedstock.

What is the difference between LPG and LNG?

LPG is mainly propane and butane and is liquefied under pressure. LNG is natural gas (mainly methane) cooled to about −162 °C to become liquid.

Can you supply a specific propane/butane ratio?

LPG mixes are quoted to the buyer’s specification. Please include the required ratio and specification in your enquiry.

Do you publish LPG prices?

No. Prices are confirmed in writing for each enquiry, based on specification, volume, destination and market conditions.
